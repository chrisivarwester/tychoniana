Methodology

Inclusion: Tycho Brahe's Latin elegy 'In effigiem Cl. Ptolemaei Alexandrini' (incipit 'Sic oculos, faciem, barbam, sic membra gerebat'), sixteen elegiac distichs dated 1 October 1584 (Kal. Octob.), composed as the verse inscription beneath the painted Ptolemy in the Uraniborg Museum and printed by Tycho in the Epistolae astronomicae (1596), p. 239 (= Opera omnia VI 269-270). This census counts the single-sheet witnesses that transmit the text outside the Epistolae: G. L. Frobenius's 1644 Almagest proof-sheet (Hamburg), and any further separate printing should one be located. Copies of the Epistolae — every one of which carries the text at p. 239 — are censused under brahe-epistolae-1596 and are not counted here. First published as a separate text by Mogens Broendsted, Fund og Forskning 9 (1962), pp. 72-78, who took it for unknown; corrected in Fund og Forskning 12 (1965), p. 156. Excluded: Copies of the Epistolae astronomicae (1596 and its 1601 Nuremberg / 1610 Frankfurt re-issues) and of Resen's Inscriptiones Hafnienses (1668), which transmit the text as part of larger books (see brahe-epistolae-1596). Frobenius's surrounding 1644 Ptolemy-translation paratexts (his own title-page, the fictive Ptolemy supplication, Longomontanus's 1626 preface, Frobenius's Regna-firmat-pietas carmen to Christian IV) — those are Frobenius's work, not Tycho's. The painted Ptolemy board of the Uraniborg Museum, under which the verses stood, is context, not a witness. Counted as a copy: Any physical exemplar of a separate printing of the elegy, should one ever be located; OR a surviving single-sheet witness carrying Tycho's elegy text. The 1644 Frobenius proof-sheet is the sole located witness of that kind. The copies

1. Stockholm, Kungliga Biblioteket — Frobenius proof-sheet (1644)

brahe-elegia-ptolemaeus-1584/stockholm-kb-frobenius-proofsheet-1644

Provenance: G.L. Frobenius (Hamburg) (Printed by Frobenius as the opening sheet of his planned Latin Almagest translation, dedicated to Christian IV; Tycho's 1584 elegy reprinted on the last page.); probably Joergen Seefeld (library dispersed to Sweden) (Binding lacks the Seefeld ex-libris but is contemporary (kin to a Hamburg 1643 binding at KB Copenhagen); Brøndsted infers a Seefeld → Stockholm path, year unstated. Seefeld's library went to Sweden in 1658.); Kungliga Biblioteket, Stockholm (Held at the Swedish Royal Library; microfilmed for KB Copenhagen in 1962.).

The one single-sheet witness to Tycho Brahe's elegy in praise of Ptolemy outside the books that transmit it. The text itself was printed by Tycho in the Epistolarum astronomicarum liber primus (Uraniborg 1596), p. 239, as the verses beneath the painted Ptolemy in the Uraniborg Museum (= Opera omnia VI 269–270), and reprinted by Resen, Inscriptiones Hafnienses (1668), p. 338. Here it is carried by a **proof-sheet (prøveark) of 1644** — the opening folio gathering of Georg Ludwig Frobenius's never-completed Latin translation of Ptolemy's Almagest (Claudii Ptolemaei Magnae Constructionis Mathematicae libri tredecim … Hamburgi, ex bibliopolio Frobeniano). Frobenius, who had spent some six weeks on Hven in 1591, set Tycho's sixteen distichs (thirty-two lines, incipit Sic oculos, faciem, barbam, sic membra gerebat) on the final page; the wording is that of the Epistolae in every line, the spelling only differing, so the sheet is a reprint from the published book, not a Hven or Wandsbek transmission.

The sheet (Brøndsted 1962, pp. 76–77) carries a title dedicated to Christian IV, a fictive Latin supplication from 'Ptolemy' citing Tycho's poem and asking for support 'som ikke vil overstige 2000 kejserlige daler', the recommendation of the translation by Jørgen Seefeld, the hofjunker Henrik Qualen, the generalprovst Jacob Fabricius and Longomontanus, and Longomontanus's preface, dated 1626. The copy is at the Kungliga Biblioteket, Stockholm; Brøndsted records that it was microfilmed for the Royal Library in Copenhagen in 1962. Its contemporary binding — kin to a Hamburg 1643 binding in Copenhagen, and bound with Frobenius's Regna firmat pietas carmen to Christian IV — lacks the Seefeld ex-libris; Brøndsted nonetheless infers a Seefeld → Stockholm path, year unstated (Seefeld's library went to Sweden in 1658).

Brøndsted published the sheet in 1962 as a hitherto unknown poem omitted from the Opera omnia; Fund og Forskning XII (1965), p. 156, corrected this in print. Grade B — the witness and its text are secure, but the Stockholm shelfmark was not recorded by Brøndsted and remains to be confirmed.

Open questions

  • Exact Kungliga Biblioteket (Stockholm) shelfmark not given by Broendsted 1962 — resolve via Libris / KB Stockholm OPAC (acquisition-queue).
  • Confirm the Seefeld provenance and whether the KB Copenhagen 1962 microfilm is digitised/available.
  • Frobenius's exemplar: the Uraniborg 1596 Epistolae, or the Nuremberg 1601 / Frankfurt 1610 re-issue? The wording is identical in all three; only a setting-level comparison of the 1644 sheet's orthography could tell.
  • The decorated initial of the first line on the 1644 sheet: S ('Sic', as the Epistolae and Resen read) — confirm on the Stockholm sheet or on the 1962 microfilm at the Royal Library, Copenhagen.

2. The conjectured 1584 Uraniborg occasional print — no separate printing attested

brahe-elegia-ptolemaeus-1584/lost-uraniborg-original-1584

Provenance: no exemplar — the text survives in the Epistolae (1596), p. 239, Resen 1668 and the 1644 Frobenius sheet (Brøndsted 1962, p. 76, conjectured a 1584–85 Uraniborg occasional sheet behind the 1644 reprint; no witness implies one. The text was printed by Tycho in the Epistolae astronomicae (1596), p. 239, as the verses beneath the painted Ptolemy in the Uraniborg Museum (= TBOO VI 269–270); the 1644 Frobenius sheet reprints that text.).

No exemplar of any separate 1584 Uraniborg printing is known, and no source implies one. The elegy — dated 1 October 1584 (Kal. Octob.) and cast as a verse inscription beneath a portrait of Ptolemy — is the text Tycho himself printed in the Epistolarum astronomicarum liber primus (Uraniborg 1596), p. 239, inside his description of the Uraniborg Museum, as the verses beneath the painted Ptolemy on the Museum wall; the Copernicus pendant of 2 October 1584 follows on p. 240 (Dreyer, Opera omnia VI 269–271). Resen reprinted it in Inscriptiones Hafnienses (1668), p. 338, and Georg Ludwig Frobenius set it again on the last page of his 1644 Almagest proof-sheet — a reprint from the published Epistolae, the wording of all thirty-two lines identical and only the spelling differing.

Brøndsted (Fund og Forskning IX, 1962, p. 76) conjectured that the poem had been run off as one of the occasional sheets with which Tycho was feeding his new press in the winter of 1584–85 and that Frobenius reproduced such a broadsheet; the Efterslæt in Fund og Forskning XII (1965), p. 156, pointed out in print that the poem stands in the Epistolae. The 1584 date is the date of composition of a verse under a Museum painting, not of a printing. The copy-slot is kept (grade D) so that the conjecture stays visible and dispositioned rather than silently dropped; it is not counted as a copy.

Open questions

  • No witness implies a separate broadside; the 1584 date is the composition date of a verse under a Museum painting (the Copernicus pendant on the next page of the Epistolae is dated 2 October 1584). Brøndsted 1962, p. 76, conjectured an occasional print to feed the new Uraniborg press; no source attests one. Retained as a copy-slot (status lost, grade D) only so that the conjecture stays visible; dispositioned excluded in the candidate ledger (2026-10-06) and not counted as a copy.
  • Should a separate printing ever surface, its first test is the Epistolae text of p. 239: the wording of all 32 lines is identical there, in Resen 1668 and in Frobenius 1644 (spelling only differs).

Brøndsted 1962 — and the correction of 1965 (added 2026-10-06)

Mogens Brøndsted, 'Et ukendt digt af Tycho Brahe', Fund og Forskning IX (1962), pp. 71–78, published the 1644 Frobenius proof-sheet (Stockholm, Kungliga Biblioteket) as the sole witness to a hitherto unknown poem, omitted from Dreyer's Opera omnia, and conjectured (p. 76) a lost 1584 Uraniborg occasional print behind it. In fact Tycho printed the elegy himself in the Epistolae astronomicae (1596), p. 239 (= Opera omnia VI 269–270; index, vol. XV p. 16), as the verses beneath the painted Ptolemy in the Uraniborg Museum, with the Copernicus pendant of 2 October 1584 on p. 240; Resen, Inscriptiones Hafnienses (1668), p. 338, reprinted it. The unsigned 'Efterslæt' in Fund og Forskning XII (1965), p. 156 (after W. Norlind), said so in print. Prior-census count: one single-sheet witness (Brøndsted 1962). Every copy of the Epistolae (brahe-epistolae-1596) carries the text at p. 239; those copies are censused there, not here. Known erratum carried by Brøndsted: the transmission 'via Hven or Wandsbek' (p. 72) — the source is the printed Epistolae.
