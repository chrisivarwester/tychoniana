Scripta Tychonis — The writings of Tycho Brahe (1546–1601) in the edition of 2019: a catalogue, with the texts

The 57 texts that Peter Zeeberg and Finn Gredal Jensen edited for Det Danske Sprog- og Litteraturselskab beyond the correspondence — 33 records of poems and inscriptions, the Copenhagen oration of 1574, the three royal nativities, the almanac for 1573, the recipes, the polemic against John Craig, and two of the printed books given whole, De nova stella (1573) and the Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (1598), with the prefaces, poems and descriptions that frame the third, the Epistolae astronomicae (1596), whose letters are in the Epistolarium. For each text: its form and its date with the evidence for it, its addressee and occasion, how it was transmitted and where it was first printed, its place in Dreyer’s Opera omnia and in the earlier catalogues, how securely it is attested, and what remains open. Each text is given in full on a page of its own, the edition’s Latin with every line and paragraph numbered for citation. The Danish translations and the editors' notes are the edition's and are read at the source, linked section by section.

This is a catalogue of the 57 texts of Tycho Brahe that the Danish critical edition of his writings prints beyond the correspondence: the web edition prepared by Peter Zeeberg and Finn Gredal Jensen for Det Danske Sprog- og Litteraturselskab in 2019. The edition's selection is the catalogue's scope. It holds the poems and inscriptions, the Copenhagen oration of 1574, the three royal nativities, the almanac for 1573, the medical compositions, the polemic against John Craig, two of the printed books entire — De nova stella (1573) and the Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (1598) — and the poems, dedication, prefaces and descriptions that frame the third, the Epistolae astronomicae (1596), whose letters are read in the Epistolarium. What the edition does not hold — the comet treatise of 1588, the Progymnasmata, the star catalogue, the verse Tycho printed inside his letters and his other books — is listed with its place in Dreyer's Opera omnia and its home in this portal in §11.

For each text the catalogue states what it is, when and on what evidence it was written, for whom and on what occasion, by which channel it reached us and where it was first printed, its place in Dreyer's Opera omnia and in the earlier bibliographies, how securely the record is attested, and what remains open. Each text is then given in full on a page of its own — the edition's Latin, section by section, with the page breaks of the Opera omnia marked, every line and paragraph numbered for citation — as the object layer of this chapter. The books as printed objects are Typographica's; the letters are the Epistolarium's; the persons are Familia's; the places are Loca's; the instruments the Mechanica describes are Instrumenta's. This catalogue bridges to each of them and duplicates none.

The record is uneven, and the catalogue says so. 29 of the 57 records carry a full apparatus — summary, occasion, addressee, printing, transmission, references and open questions, graded on their evidence; the other 28 carry their identifying fields, the edition's own data and the computed fields, and no grade. The two states are marked on every record and in the catalogue table.
