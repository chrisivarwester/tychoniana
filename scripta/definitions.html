How to read an entry

What a text is here. A record is one book of the edition's series that is not a Danish translation of another book in it: the edition publishes 67 web books, of which 10 are Danish translations shown here beside their Latin, and one (Occasio et argumentum) is a Danish translation whose Latin the edition does not print and which therefore stands as a text of its own. The record is the edition's unit, not a count of Tycho's works: four records are editorial groupings of several pieces (four epigrams on the death of Frederik II; nine Uraniborg inscriptions; two Kronborg inscriptions; three recipes), one is the second recension of another record's poem, two are supplements to texts held elsewhere, four are prefaces or dedications, one is Tycho's translation of a poem by James VI, and two are whole printed books whose sections include pieces by other hands — a poem by Hans Frandsen, a letter of Pratensis, a poem by Anders Sørensen Vedel, the printer's note; Holger Rosenkrantz's poem, two letters of Jacob Kurz, two of Magini, an ode by Rudolphus Caukerchius. Every such section is headed with its author on the text page and in the sections table.

Numbering and order. Records are numbered 1–57 in the order of the register: the three periods of Tycho's life in sequence, and within each the date of composition, undated last. The number is stable and is the citation key together with the record's own key, which is the edition's slug (brahe-t_…) and the address every other page of this portal uses for the text.

Sections. A text page is divided as the edition divides it, one section per page of the web edition, numbered from the title page as section 1. The edition's own chapter numbers, where it prints them, are shown beside the section number: section 12 of De nova stella is the edition's chapter 11. The section number is what the citation and the links from the other pillars carry.

Forms and channels. Each record carries one of 18 forms (defined in the table below) and, where it is established, the channel by which the text reached us: printed in one of Tycho's own books, separately printed at his order, printed by others in his lifetime, printed after his death, or in manuscript until the Opera omnia — Zeeberg's own account of the transmission, as this catalogue partitions it. A channel not yet established is left blank, never guessed.

Dates. Every date carries its basis and a certainty mark: secure where the text dates itself or two authorities agree; probable on one authority or an inference from the content; uncertain where authorities disagree or the bounds are wide; unknown where no source dates the text and the edition's year is a placement; the edition's where the edition's year has not been independently examined. Where research has corrected the edition's year, both are shown and the correction is stated in words; the 15 cases are gathered in §9.

Grades. A researched record is graded A–D on the evidence behind it, on the scale of the other pillars (A secure, B probable, C uncertain, D speculative). A record not yet researched carries no grade: its status is a state of work, not a judgement.

Text. The Latin is the edition's constituted text. Superscript figures on a text page are the edition's textual notes where this catalogue reproduces them; the page breaks of the Opera omnia and the witnesses' folios are marked inline. Prose is set in numbered paragraphs, verse in numbered lines with the pentameter indented; the four passages of De nova stella whose verses the web text runs together are set again in lines here, after the line breaks of Dreyer's edition, and each carries the note. A text of forty lines or fewer is printed entire in its register entry as well as on its own page.

What the figures are not

The count of 57 texts is the edition's row count and nothing more: it neither counts Tycho's works nor measures his output. 33 records of verse and inscriptions hold 45 separately transmitted pieces; the edition omits three pieces of Dreyer's Carmina and all the verse Dreyer prints inside the other volumes (§11), so no ratio of these figures is a completeness rate. "Given in full" means the edition's text: the figures of the 1573 almanac, the nativity charts and the woodcuts of the Mechanica are transmitted by the edition as placeholders and are not reproduced here; the Mechanica's woodcuts are seen in Instrumenta. The Epistolae astronomicae is given in its non-letter parts; its letters are the Epistolarium's. "In Tycho's own Danish" marks one text, the 1591 preface printed in Flemløs's book, which the edition prints among Tycho's writings although it speaks in Flemløs's name; the attribution is the edition's. The Danish beside the Latin is Peter Zeeberg's translation of 2019 where the edition names him, the edition's translation where it does not, and, for the Mechanica's instrument chapters, an older translation reproduced by the edition in a pre-1948 orthography whose translator the local copy does not name. Every text here but one is Latin; he also wrote Danish (the Flemløs preface, his letters home) and German (the comet tract of 1577, not in the edition of 2019).

The edition and its layers

Tycho's words are in the public domain, and so is the text of Dreyer's Opera omnia (1913–1929) on which the edition rests. The edition of 2019 adds four layers that are Det Danske Sprog- og Litteraturselskab's and the editors': the constituted Latin, which re-collates Dreyer's text against the witnesses; the editors' textual notes; Peter Zeeberg's explanatory commentary and biographical notes; and the Danish translations. This build reproduces the constituted Latin only. The layers it does not reproduce are read at the source, to which every text page links section by section; each page states, for every layer, what it carries and what it withholds. The Society's permission for the republication of its layers has been asked for and is recorded here when it is given; until then the translations and the notes stay at the source, and the commentary is never reproduced.
