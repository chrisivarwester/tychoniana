The original

MAGNIFICO ET NOBILISSIMO VIRO, D. TICHONI BRAHE, MATHEMATICO S. C. MAIESTATIS EXCELLENTISSIMO, AMICO ET PATRONO PLURIMUM OBSERVANDO.
MAGNIFICE AC NOBILISSIME DOMINE TICHO, AMICE HONORANDE.
S.

CUM certa per magnificum Dominum IOANNEM GEORGIUM GODELMANNUM mihi scribendi occasio daretur, plenius scribendum duxi. Vivo, ut possum, quando iniquitas temporis et fortunæ maiora spectare non sinit. Calamitas belli Carolini oppressit meam patriam. Nam Dux CAROLUS totam fere occupavit Livoniam. Sed qua felicitate, eventus docebit. Novisti illius tyranni dictum: aliena appetendo propria amisit. Rex statusque Poloniæ de bello per triennium magnis sumptibus gerendo in Comitijs convenerunt. Funesta ergo omnia in mea patria sunt. Quanta interim animi virtute et constantia opus sit mihi, facile intelligis, qui et bonis omnibus et maximis sumptibus propterea factis excidi. Spem tamen reliquam in DEI auxilio fixam habeo, fore ut postquam nubila et turbulenta ista tempora transierunt, pax florentissima et quies nobis restituatur. Interim tu me benevolentia et fide prosequaris singulari velim.

Quæ ad D. CHYTRÆUM de tuæ vitæ historia scripsisti, heredes eius mihi tradiderunt, quæ plene meis historicis meditationibus inseram, nec alia tui ornandi argumenta omittam. Nam te virum bonum et vere syncerum cognovi, quæ virtutes profecto pulcherrimam istam artem tuam, in qua præcellis, elegantissime illuminant. Quod vero tardius liber meus historiarum prodit, multa in causa sunt, quia et initium cuiusque rei grave est, et veritatem sequi in omnibus hoc seculo scis quam sit periculosum. Deinde alijs adhuc occupor elaborandis. Mox tamen lucem videbunt, ubi meo genio ita visum fuerit.

Vale interim, vir optime, et magnam salutem tuos domesticos meo nomine imperti.
Datæ Rostochio 10 die Martij A. 1601.
Tui amantiss.
CHRISTOPH. STURCIUS.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the magnificent and most noble man, Master Tycho Brahe, most excellent Mathematician of His Imperial Majesty, friend and patron much to be observed. Magnificent and most noble Master Tycho, friend to be honoured. Greeting.

Since a sure occasion of writing to me was given through the magnificent Master Johann Georg Gödelmann, I judged I should write more fully. I live as I can, since the iniquity of the times and of fortune does not permit [me] to look to greater things. The calamity of the Carolinian war has oppressed my homeland. For Duke Charles has occupied almost all Livonia. But with what good fortune, the outcome will teach. You know that tyrant's saying: "by coveting what is another's, he lost his own." The King and the Estates of Poland have agreed, in the Diet, on waging war for three years at great expense. All things, therefore, are deadly in my homeland. With how great virtue and constancy of mind I meanwhile have need, you easily understand — [I] who have fallen from all my goods, and from the greatest expenses made on that account. Yet I have my remaining hope fixed in God's help, that — after these cloudy and turbulent times have passed — a most flourishing peace and quiet may be restored to us. Meanwhile, I would wish that you pursue me with singular benevolence and faith.

What you wrote to Master Chytraeus about the history of your life, his heirs have handed over to me; which I will fully insert into my historical meditations, nor will I omit other matters for adorning you. For I have known you as a good and truly sincere man — which virtues indeed most elegantly illuminate that most beautiful art of yours, in which you excel. But that my book of histories comes forth rather slowly, there are many causes: both because the beginning of anything is hard, and because you know how dangerous it is, in this age, to follow truth in all things; then, I am still occupied in elaborating other [works]. Soon, however, they will see the light, when it shall so seem [good] to my disposition.

Farewell meanwhile, best man, and impart a great greeting to your household in my name.
Given at Rostock, the 10th day of March, in the year 1601.
Most loving of you,
Christoph Sturtz.

[Postscript:] I append the oration delivered by me on Sigismund August, of blessed memory.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Christoph Sturtz (Sturcius), the Rostock historian — the Sturtzius, historicus vester whom Tycho names in brahe-t_16001116003 as a recipient of his autobiographical material. The witness confirms the link: Sturtz writes that Chytraeus's heirs have given him "what you wrote to Master [David] Chytraeus about the history of your life," which he will insert into his own histories (the same biographical notice Tycho discussed in the Brasch letter).
  • The Livonian war — Sturtz's ruined homeland. Calamitas belli Carolini oppressit meam patriam … Dux Carolus totam fere occupavit Livoniam — Sturtz's homeland is Livonia, ravaged by the war of Duke Charles (the future Charles IX of Sweden) against Poland-Lithuania over Livonia (1600–01); the Polish king and Diet have voted "three years of war at great expense." Sturtz has lost his property. (The same Swedish-Polish conflict Tycho comments on in brahe-t_16000329002.) aliena appetendo propria amisit — "by coveting another's he lost his own" (the Phaedrus/Aesopic moral, applied to "that tyrant," i.e. Duke Charles).
  • D. Godelmannus = Johann Georg Gödelmann, the Saxon legate and reliable courier (cf. brahe-t_15981112001, …16010308001).
  • Calendar. Rostochio 10 die Martij 1601 = 10 March 1601; Rostock (Lutheran) = Old Style. Header agrees with the label.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Final line after the signature "CHRISTOPH. STURCIUS.", restored from Dreyer VIII p.403 (dropped by the working transcription).
