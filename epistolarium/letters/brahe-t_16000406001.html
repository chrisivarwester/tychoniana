The original

ILLUSTRI ET GENEROSO DOMINO, DOMINO IOHANNI FRIDERICO HOFMANNO, LIBERO BARONI IN GRUENPUEHEL ET STRECHAU, PRÆFECTO PROVINCIÆ DUCATUS STYRIÆ ET ARCHIDUCATUS AUSTRIÆ, MARSCHALCHO UBIQUE HÆREDITARIO, BURGRAVIO IN STEYER, NEC NON SACRÆ CÆSAREÆ MAIESTATIS AULÆ CONSILIARIO ET SERENISSIMI ARCHIDUCIS MAXIMILIANI CUBICULARIO PRIMARIO, DOMINO ET AMICO SUO IMPRIMIS HONORANDO.

ILLUSTRIS ET GENEROSE VIR, DOMINE ET AMICE HONORANDISSIME.

REDIT nunc Pragam M. KEPLERUS, et licet suasor illi fuerim, ut domestici mei, qui etiamnum Pilsnæ est, et inter alia in negocio dictum KEPLERUM concernente sollicitat, reditum, quem intra paucos dies futurum spero, expectaret, ut quid fieri possit, certius resciremus (præsertim cum spes aliqua facta sit, Saam Cæsam Matem petitionibus meis humilimis clementissime annuere velle) atque demum aliquid rati concludere possemus: tamen cum ipsum nihilominus abitum festinare intelligerem, reliqui hominem suo arbitrio. Quid vero inter nos ventilatum sit, Illris Do Tua e D. Doctore IOANNE IESSENIO, qui has adfert, seorsim cognoscet: is enim interfuit, dum hæc gererentur: quare longiore et tædiosâ eorum commemoratione supersedeo, omnia ad hanc rem facientia Illustris Dnis Tuæ prudenti consilio dispicienda ordinandaque etiamnum relinquens.

Cœterum de puero, quem Illustris Dnatio Tua ad me mittere decrevit, sciat me libenter eum, quandocunque advenerit, recepturum, tum quoque laboratorem Spagiricum. Et expecto pariter fabrum illum Mechanicum, qui e Styria venturus est. Quam primum vero per occupationes licuerit, spero me Pragæ futurum, ubi plenius cum Illri Done Tua de ijs, quæ hîc omissa sunt, agam.

Valeat Tua Illris Do quam optime et diutissime.
Dabantur ex arce Cæsarea Benatica die 6 Aprilis Anno 1600.
Illri Generositati Tuæ
Addictissimus
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the illustrious and generous Lord, Lord Johann Friedrich Hoffmann, Free Baron in Grünbühel and Strechau, Prefect of the Province of the Duchy of Styria and of the Archduchy of Austria, hereditary Marshal everywhere, Burgrave in Steyr, and also Councillor of the Court of His Sacred Imperial Majesty and First Chamberlain of the Most Serene Archduke Maximilian, his lord and friend especially to be honoured.

Illustrious and generous man, lord and most honoured friend.

Master Kepler now returns to Prague; and although I had been his advisor [urged him] to await the return of my servant — who is still at Pilsen, and, among other things, is pressing [matters] in the business concerning the said Kepler — which return I hope will be within a few days, so that we might more certainly find out what can be done (especially since some hope has been given that His Imperial Majesty wishes most clemently to assent to my most humble petitions), and so that at last we might conclude something settled: yet, since I understood that he was nonetheless hastening his departure, I left the man to his own judgment. But what was ventilated between us, Your Illustrious Lordship will learn separately from Doctor Jan Jessenius, who brings these [lines]; for he was present while these things were transacted. Wherefore I forbear a longer and tedious recounting of them, leaving everything bearing on this matter still to be considered and ordered by Your Illustrious Lordship's prudent counsel.

For the rest, about the boy whom Your Illustrious Lordship has decided to send to me, let [you] know that I will gladly receive him, whenever he arrives — and likewise the spagyric [alchemical] laborator. And I equally await that mechanical craftsman who is to come from Styria. As soon as my occupations permit, I hope to be at Prague, where I will more fully treat with Your Illustrious Lordship about those things which have been omitted here.

May Your Illustrious Lordship fare as well and as long as possible.
Given from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 6th day of April, in the year 1600.
Most devoted to Your Illustrious Generosity,
Tycho Brahe,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • A key Tycho–Kepler document — preserved in Kepler's own hand. The witness (Vienna, cod. Vindob. lat. 10703) is a transcript made by Kepler himself, which is why it survives among the Keplerian papers. It dates from the first, strained Benátky period (Feb–April 1600), when Kepler had come to Tycho but the terms of his stay were unsettled.
  • Recipient. Johann Friedrich Hoffmann, Free Baron of Grünbühel and Strechau (1568–1615), the great Styrian magnate — Landeshauptmann of Styria, hereditary Marshal, Burgrave of Steyr, Imperial Aulic Councillor, and First Chamberlain of Archduke Maximilian — and Kepler's patron, at whose Prague house Kepler lodged. Tycho writes to him as the broker of Kepler's situation.
  • Content. Kepler "now returns to Prague"; Tycho had urged him to wait for the return (from Pilsen, where the court was) of Tycho's servant, who was "pressing the business concerning Kepler" — i.e. seeking the Emperor's assent to Tycho's petitions (a salaried place for Kepler) — "especially since some hope" had been given that Rudolf II would "most clemently assent"; but Kepler hastened off, so Tycho "left the man to his own judgment." The details of "what was ventilated between us" Hoffmann will hear from the bearer, Dr. Jan Jessenius (the anatomist, present at the negotiations). This is contemporary evidence of the delicate Tycho–Kepler accommodation that Jessenius helped mediate.
  • Other matters. Hoffmann is sending Tycho a puer (a boy/page), a laborator Spagiricus (an alchemical laboratory-assistant — spagyric = Paracelsian chemistry), and a faber Mechanicus (a mechanic/instrument-maker) "from Styria" — staffing for Tycho's Prague establishment.
  • Calendar. ex arce Caesarea Benatica die 6 Aprilis 1600 = 6 April 1600; Bohemia (imperial) = New Style. Header agrees with the label.
