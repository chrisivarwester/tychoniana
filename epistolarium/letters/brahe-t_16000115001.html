The original

GENEROSO VEREQVE NOBILI VIRO DN. TYCHONI BRAHE DE KNUDSTRUP, AVUNCULO MEO CARISSIMO.

DE tuo mei amore, quo indeusque a teneris annis large sum usus, tuisqve beneficiis, qvibus me semper complexus es, avuncule carissime, gratias, ut par est, ago qvam maximas, polliceorqve, si mea unqvam extare possunt officiola, in te constabunt promptissima paratissimaqve. Ne avtem mirere, avuncule carissime, qvod pavlo tardior in literis hactenus fui, siqvidem variis ego rebus cogitationibusqve, de instituenda præsertim peregrinatione, varie sum occupatus, qvibus tamen, avxiliante omnipotenti Deo, decimus tertius dies mensis decembris proxime absoluti anni, felicem expectatumqve finem imposuit; fore itaqve spero, ut diligentius post hac, qvoties per occasionem licuerit, literis tecum de meis rebus agam, certo enim certius mecum statuo, te de mea salute æqve solicitum atqve de genuini nati. Verum ut cognoscas, ubinam gentium ubiqve terrarum nunc vivam, scito, me cum comite mei itineris studiorumqve Nicolao Mathiæ Bachendorphio ad nominatum diem (cum tamen recta Basilæam decrevissem) Heidelbergam pervenisse, ubi propter urentis frigoris vehementiam usqve ad vernas nundinas proxime instantes Francofurdinas anchoram figere visum fuit, factumqve est integris, lavs Deo, omnibus. In itinere avtem meo comitata me est mater mea svavissima, soror tua perqvam dilecta, Brunsvigam usqve, ubi post pavcos dies obvium habuit Ericum Lange, qvi cum ipsa, ut puto, in Cimbriam iter fecit. Plura nec tempus nec tabellarii festinatio in præsentiarum scribere permittit. Velis ergo per pavca hæc boni consulere, alias enim majora dabo.

Vale.
Heidelberga 18 cal. feb. a:o 1600.
Tacho Tot O. f.
mea manu.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the generous and truly noble man, Master Tycho Brahe of Knudstrup, my dearest [maternal] uncle.

For your love toward me — which I have enjoyed largely from my tender years up to this point — and for your benefits, with which you have always embraced me, dearest uncle, I give, as is fitting, the greatest thanks, and promise that, if my poor little services can ever be of any account, they will stand toward you most ready and most prepared. But do not wonder, dearest uncle, that I have hitherto been somewhat tardy in [my] letters, since I have been variously occupied with various matters and deliberations — especially about the undertaking of a journey [study-tour]; to which, however, with omnipotent God's help, the thirteenth day of the month of December of the just-completed year put a happy and hoped-for end. So I hope that hereafter I shall the more diligently, as often as occasion allows, treat with you by letter about my affairs; for I hold it more certain than certain that you are as anxious about my welfare as about that of a son of your own begetting. But that you may know in what nation, in what part of the world, I now live: know that I, with the companion of my journey and studies, Niels Mathiesen Bachendorph, arrived on the said day at Heidelberg (although I had decided [to go] straight to Basel), where, on account of the violence of the burning cold, it seemed good to cast anchor until the next coming Frankfurt spring fair; and it was done with all of us whole [in health], praise God. On my journey my sweetest mother — your most beloved sister — accompanied me as far as Brunswick, where, after a few days, she met Erik Lange, with whom, as I think, she made the journey into Cimbria [Holstein]. More, neither the time nor the courier's haste permits [me] to write at present. Be willing, therefore, to take these few [words] in good part; for otherwise I will give greater [ones].

Farewell.
Heidelberg, the 18th [day] before the Kalends of February [15 January], in the year 1600.
Tage Thott, son of Otte,
by my [own] hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender — Tycho's nephew. Tage Ottesen Thott (1580–1658), the Danish nobleman, son of Otte Thott and of Sophie Brahe — Tycho's astronomer-sister. He addresses Tycho as avunculus carissimus ("dearest maternal uncle"), and his mother as "your most beloved sister" — fixing the family triangle. He signs Tacho Tot O[ttonis] f[ilius], "Tage Thott, son of Otte."
  • The study-tour. Tage, with his tutor-companion Niels Mathiesen Bachendorph, on a peregrinatio academica; he had meant to go "straight to Basel" but, the cold being fierce, wintered at Heidelberg until the Frankfurt spring fair.
  • Sophie Brahe and Erik Lange — a notable family datum. mater mea … soror tua … comitata me … Brunsvigam usque, ubi … obvium habuit Ericum Lange, qui cum ipsa … in Cimbriam iter fecit — Tycho's sister Sophie Brahe accompanied her son as far as Brunswick, where she met Erik Lange (the alchemist, her long-betrothed and later husband), with whom she travelled "into Cimbria" (the Jutland/Holstein peninsula). A direct contemporary witness to the famous Sophie Brahe–Erik Lange union (Lange's debts had kept them apart for years).
  • Calendar. Heidelberga 18 cal. feb. a:o 1600 = ante diem XVIII Kalendas Februarii = 15 January 1600; Heidelberg (Calvinist Palatinate) kept the Julian / Old Style calendar. The internal "thirteenth day of December of the just-completed year" is the O.S. date on which his travel-plans were settled. Header agrees with the label (15 January).
