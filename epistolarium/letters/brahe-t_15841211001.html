The original

THYGGE BRAHE FICH BREFF NOGENN COMPASSER ANRØRE[N]DIS VT SEQUITUR

Frederich etc.

WOR gunst thilforn, Wij kunde Oß andet icke ehrindre, end att Wij nu nogit forleden haffue thil thig offuerantuordet en Wor compaß, som thu selff haffde ladit Oß giøre, huilcken war bleffuen nogit wrichthig, attu therfore Oß thend schulle lade egien giøre thil rette. Ther som nu saa er schet, och samme compas er ferdig giort, tha bede Wij thig, attu Oß thend wforsømmit thilschicker, thersom thu och jcke for:ne compaß haffuer bekommit, tha bede Wij thig och begere, attu wil lade Oß giøre tho compaßer, saadanne som thend anden war, och thett kunstrichs och restist schie kand, saa Wij thennum met thett første mueligt wor kunde bekomme.

Ther meth etc.
Actum Soer, xj decembr: aar 84.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tyge Brahe received a letter concerning certain compasses, as follows:

Frederick, etc.

Our favour beforehand. We cannot but call to mind that we did, some while ago now, hand over to you one of our compasses — which you yourself had had made for us, [and] which had become somewhat inaccurate — so that you were therefore to have it put right again for us. If it has now been so done, and that same compass is finished, then we ask you to send it to us without fail; and if you have not [yet] got the aforesaid compass [back in hand], then we ask and desire you to have two compasses made for us, such as the other one was, and made as artfully and as accurately as can be, so that we may obtain them at the very first that may be possible.

Therewith, etc.
Done at Sorø, 11 December, year [15]84.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Frederik II of Denmark (r. 1559–88), Tycho's royal patron, who in 1576 had granted him the island of Hven. A characteristic instance of the king treating Tycho as his instrument-maker as well as his astronomer.
  • Document type. A chancery-register entry (Sjællandske Tegnelser). The Latin rubric "…NOGENN COMPASSER ANRØRENDIS ut sequitur" ("concerning certain compasses, as follows") heads the Danish body — a code-switch preserved by this chapter's convention ANRØRE[N]DIS: the source contracts anrørendes with a tilde for the nasal; expanded in brackets.
  • compaß / compasser — rendered "compass(es)." Ambiguity flagged: in 1580s Danish royal usage this is most likely a drawing/measuring compass (pair of compasses, dividers) of fine workmanship — the king stresses it be made kunstrigt og rigtigst ("as artfully and accurately as can be") and that Tycho had had it made — but the word can also denote a sundial-/magnetic compass-dial. The text does not decide it; "compass" is kept and the ambiguity recorded rather than resolved.
  • Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1584). WOR gunst thilforn = Vor gunst tilforn ("Our favour beforehand," the royal address-formula, this chapter's convention); attu = at du; offuerantuordet = overantvordet ("handed over/delivered"); wrichthig = urigtig ("inaccurate, out of true"); wforsømmit = uforsømmet ("without fail/neglect"); for:ne = fornævnte ("aforesaid," the standard chancery abbreviation); kunstrichs = kunstrigt ("artfully, skilfully"); restist = rigtigst ("most accurately/correctly"); thennum = dennem ("them").
  • Calendar / place. Actum Soer, xj decembr. aar 84 = "Done at Sorø, 11 December [15]84" (O.S.); Actum "Done/Given" kept by this chapter's convention
