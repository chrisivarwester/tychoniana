The original

DEM EDLEN VNNDT GESTRENGEN RITTER HERRN TYCHO BRAHE ZU KNUDSTORP MEYNEM SONDERS GUNSTIGEN VND VIELGELIEBTEN HERRENN.
TRESEN ODER WITTENBERGK.

ILLUSTRIS ac Clarissime eques et Domine obserme. Sacra Caesarea Maiestas Dominus noster Clementissimus mandavit mihi, ut Illustrem Dominationem tuam statim huc vocem, cui hisce meis humillime obsequi et debui et volui. Curet itaque Dominatio Tua Illustris quam diligentissime, ut quam primum poterit, se ad iter accingat, sicque coram Caesare hîc Pragae statuat, ut is hac in parte in Dominatione tua illustri, vel etiam me ipso, ne minimum quidem defuisse animadvertat. Porro de hac Illustris D. T. personae Augusta evocatione non tam D. T. Illri gratulor, quam mihi ipsi. Mirum in modum gaudeo, utpote qui hac occasione, te tantum ac talem virum et praeter generis nobilitatem, omni virtutum et literarum genere exornatum atque excultum coram videre tuisque doctissimis sermonibus quandoque frui potero. Vale quam optime et diutissime, Vir Nobilissime, omnemque adhibe diligentiam, ut nobis salvus atque incolumis huc advenias.

Datae Pragae die 27. Ianuarij Anno 1599, quem tibi cum multis alijs felicissimum a Deo ter Optimo Maximo precor.
Illustri atque Clariss. D. T. deditissimus
RUDOLPHIUS CORADUCIUS,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the noble and stern [valiant] knight, Master Tycho Brahe of Knudstrup, my especially favourable and dearly-beloved Lord. At Dresden or Wittenberg.

Illustrious and most distinguished knight and most observed Lord. His Sacred Imperial Majesty, our most clement Lord, has commanded me to call Your Illustrious Lordship hither at once; which [command], by these my [letters], I both ought and wished most humbly to obey. Let Your Illustrious Lordship therefore see to it, as diligently as possible, that, as soon as you can, you gird yourself for the journey, and so present yourself before the Emperor here at Prague, that he may perceive that in this matter nothing at all — not even the least — has been wanting, whether on Your Illustrious Lordship's part or on my own. Furthermore, on this august summoning of Your Illustrious Lordship's person, I congratulate not so much Your Illustrious Lordship as myself. I rejoice in a marvellous manner, inasmuch as on this occasion I shall be able to see in person — and sometime to enjoy the most learned discourses of — you, so great and such a man, who, besides nobility of birth, are adorned and cultivated with every kind of virtues and of letters. Fare most well and most long, most noble Man, and apply all diligence to come hither to us safe and unharmed.

Given at Prague, the 27th day of January, in the year 1599 — which [year] I pray, from God thrice Best and Greatest, may be most happy for you, together with many others [to come].
Most devoted to Your Illustrious and most distinguished Lordship,
Rudolf Coraduz,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • The imperial summons. This is the formal letter calling Tycho to Prague — written at the express command of Emperor Rudolf II ("His Sacred Imperial Majesty … commanded me to call Your Illustrious Lordship hither at once") — the document that drew Tycho into the imperial service. It directly answers Gödelmann's autumn advice (brahe-t_15981112001) to wait at Dresden or Wittenberg: the German address indeed directs the letter "to Dresden or Wittenberg" (Tresen oder Wittenbergk), where Tycho was wintering.
  • Sender. Rudolf Coraduz (Rudolphus Coraducius), Imperial Vice-Chancellor (Reichsvizekanzler; the Procancellarius of brahe-t_15980530001, …1201001), the senior official handling Tycho's reception. He writes warmly, "congratulating himself" on the prospect of Tycho's learned company.
  • Register. Elaborate chancery-Latin courtesy: Illustris ac Clarissime eques et Domine observandissime; Dominatio Tua Illustris; Deo ter Optimo Maximo. The German address keeps the knightly style edler und gestrenger Ritter.
  • Calendar. Pragae die 27. Ianuarij 1599 = 27 January 1599; Prague (imperial) = New Style. Header agrees with the label.
  • Correction. Address heading corrected from 'VNO' to 'VND' (=und) per Dreyer, TBOO VIII p.136; a single-letter transcription typo that did not affect the published translation.
